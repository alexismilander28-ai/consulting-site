Three places the money leaks out

None of these show up on a profit and loss statement. They show up as jobs that went to someone else.

  1. Tue 6:42 pmMissed call, no message

    The call nobody answered

    The crew is up on a roof, the office closed at 4:30, the phone rings out. A caller who reaches voicemail often doesn't leave a message. They call the next shop on the list.

  2. Mon 9:15 amQuote sent, no follow-up

    The quote nobody chased

    You drove out, measured, wrote it up and sent it. Then the week got busy. A homeowner comparing three quotes often goes with whoever called back.

  3. Thu 9:30 pmInvoices, reminders, reviews

    The paperwork after dinner

    Appointment reminders, nudges on unpaid invoices, asking happy customers for a review, typing the same job into two systems. It gets done at the kitchen table, or it doesn't get done.

Straight answers

What will the AI actually do?

Less than you might think, on purpose. Most of the work is plain automation: text a caller back when a call is missed, remind your office about an open quote, send appointment and invoice reminders. AI is used where it saves real time, like drafting a reply or summarizing a voicemail, and a person on your team checks that work.

What won't it do?

It won't answer your phone pretending to be a person. It won't send a customer anything you haven't approved. It won't replace the person who runs your office; it takes the repetitive part off their desk. And I won't promise you a number of jobs or dollars. I measure before and after, so you can see what changed.

Who approves what customers see?

You do. Every automatic message is a template you approve in writing before it goes live. Anything an AI drafts is read and sent by a person on your team.

Do I have to switch software?

No. If you already pay for field-service software, the first job is getting what you have to work properly, then filling the gaps. I don't migrate or replace your systems.

What about the rules on texting customers?

Texting customers comes with rules on consent, opting out and carrier registration. When texting is part of a later project, the registration is set up under your business, you keep the opt-in records, and STOP is tested before anything sends. I'm not a lawyer, so the final word on your message wording belongs to your attorney.

How much does it cost?

The Map has a fixed fee you see in writing before you commit, tax included. Later work, when offered, gets a fixed written price plus applicable sales tax and is never billed by the hour. Software and texting fees are billed to you directly by those companies.

Do you work in Spanish?

Yes. Customer messages, staff training and the written runbook in English, Spanish, or both.

What happens to my accounts?

Nothing of yours is held by me. The Map needs no access to your accounts. If you later hire anyone to build, everything stays in your accounts and you can remove their access.

Where do you work?

Eastern and Northeastern Pennsylvania. The Map includes time in your office, in person.
