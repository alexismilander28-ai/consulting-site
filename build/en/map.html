The Map is one visit to your shop and one written report.

I come in person, watch how a call becomes a paid job, and write down where it stops. I build nothing and I change nothing.

The first thing I offer. The Map is paid. You get the cost and a short agreement in writing before you commit, and the report is yours to use with anyone.

How a visit goes

Before
We talk for about 20 minutes by phone. You tell me how calls, quotes and jobs move through your office now. We pick a day.
The day
I drive to your shop. I cover Wilkes-Barre, Scranton, Hazleton and the Allentown area. I sit with you and whoever answers the phone, for two hours or more. We count what is already there: calls nobody answered, quotes still open, appointments people missed. I take notes and record nothing. I don’t log in to anything. You show me your screens and I write down what I see.
Within two weeks
I come back and hand you the report, and we read it together. The visit and the report can be in English, in Spanish, or both.
After
You decide. Change one thing, change several, wait, or do nothing. You can hire anyone to do the work.

Two places I look first

The two examples below are samples, not real shops. They show the kind of thing the visit counts.

Tuesday, 6:42 pm. A call with no message.

The call nobody answered

The crew is up on a roof, the office closed at 4:30, and the phone rings out. A caller who reaches voicemail may not leave a message. They may call the next shop on the list.

Monday, 9:15 am. A quote sent, then nothing.

The quote nobody chased

You drove out, measured, wrote it up and sent it. Then the week got busy. A customer comparing quotes may go with whoever called back.

I also look at the paperwork that gets done at the kitchen table after dinner: reminders, unpaid invoices, typing the same job into two systems.

Of every 10 quotes you send, how many get a second call from your office?

That is one of the things the visit counts.

What the report holds

  • One page that shows how a call becomes a paid job in your shop, with the stops marked.
  • Each stop written from what I saw and what you told me. Any figure in money is an estimate and is labeled as one.
  • What I would change first, in order.
  • What should stay with a person, and why.

I make no promise of jobs or money. The report tells you what I counted.
