# Positioning opinion: is the site selling us short?

Written 2026-11-15 for Carlos. Advisory only. Nothing on the site, the host or the legal files was
changed. I am not a lawyer; where I mention a rule I am repeating what your own
`legal/compliance-review.md` and `legal/launch-gates.md` say, in plain words.

## Short answer

You are right about the wording and right that the site hides most of what exists. You are
partly wrong about what can be sold today: by your own decision of 2026-11-14 ("EIN, bank,
insurance last; no paid client work until then"), nothing paid is cleared yet, not even the visit.
The fix for the wording is free and can happen now. The fix for the offer is three or four errands,
most of them free.

My recommendation: lead with a live demo you run on a call, sell a small paid visit under your own
name as soon as the errands are done, and hold the $1,500 install until the separate company
exists. Show the visit price on the site. Do not show the install price yet.

---

## 1. Is he right?

### Where the site undersells what really exists

- **"Tested on made-up data" appears on almost every card** of the AI workers page (nine times),
  plus "none runs for a customer yet" on the home page, the AI workers page and the leave-behind.
  Said once it is honesty. Said nine times it is a warning label.
- **It is also less than the truth.** The booking flow was tested end to end on your real Google
  Calendar and your real Telegram. There are 14 workflows on a hosted system, five industry packs
  (HVAC, auto repair, barbershop, salon, and an insurance draft), English and Spanish wording for
  every message, a pause switch, failure alerts, a daily check-in and an opt-out list. "Made-up
  data" describes the sample customers, not the system. The system is real and it works.
- **The receptionist is invisible.** The website chat receptionist is one closed accordion row.
  The phone receptionist gets one line: "not offered yet". The sample call videos (booking,
  emergency, price, unknown price, in both languages) are not on the site at all.
- **The home page is all Map.** The best thing you have, Approve / Decline on your phone and the
  appointment landing on the calendar, is two clicks down inside a list.
- **About says "I build working software with AI for my own use."** That is the weakest true
  sentence available.

### (a) Honest reframes that cost nothing

| Now | Say instead | Why it is still true |
|---|---|---|
| Tested on made-up data | A working demo, built around a sample shop | The system runs; only the customers are invented |
| None runs for a customer yet | I'm taking my first customers | Same fact, said as a reason, not a confession |
| Stills of a test | I'll run it live for you on a call | You can, on your own accounts |
| Not offered yet (phone) | Built; I can play you a recorded sample call | The build and the videos exist |
| Made-up numbers, not results | Sample shop, example numbers | Keeps the label, drops the alarm |

### (b) What would cross the line

- Saying or implying it runs for customers, "businesses use this", "clients", "we", "our team".
- Any result: jobs won, calls saved, money, percentages, "never miss a call".
- Removing the sample label from any screen that shows numbers. Unlabeled numbers read as results.
- Offering the phone receptionist for sale. Pennsylvania needs everyone on a call to agree to
  recording, and your own clearance list says that wording is not settled.
- Showing reminders or invoice notices **as text messages you can install today**. No workflow
  sends a text. Texting needs the client's own registration and recorded consent first. Today
  these go by email.
- "Pilot", "beta" or "trial customers" as a label. The insurance quote lists beta-testing claims
  as an exclusion. I am not sure how far that reaches; avoid the words.

**One thing I found that goes the other way.** The AI workers page leads with a missed-call text
and calls the pictures "still pictures of a test". I could not find a workflow that sends that
text; the product notes say texting is not built. If no real test was sent from a test number,
that caption overstates. Either run one on your own number or relabel it "illustration".

none runs for a customer yet")

**4. First customers** (replaces "None is running for a customer yet"; use only once you can

- ES: Estoy empezando con mis primeros clientes. Por eso el precio es bajo y por eso hago cada
  trabajo yo mismo.

**5. Receptionist** (replaces "The phone receptionist is not offered yet")
- EN: The website receptionist is ready to show today. The phone receptionist is built, and I can
  play you a recorded sample call. I install it for a business once the call-recording notice
  Pennsylvania requires is settled in writing.
- ES: La recepcionista de la página web se la puedo mostrar hoy. La recepcionista telefónica ya
  está construida y puedo ponerle una llamada de muestra grabada. La instalo en un negocio cuando
  quede resuelto por escrito el aviso de grabación que exige Pensilvania.

**6. About** (replaces "I build working software with AI for my own use")
- EN: I built every part of this system myself and tested it on my own accounts: booking,
  reminders, quote follow-ups and a weekly report, in English and Spanish. Ask me to open any
  part and I'll show you how it works.
- ES: Construí cada parte de este sistema yo mismo y lo probé en mis propias cuentas: citas,
  recordatorios, seguimiento de presupuestos y un informe semanal, en inglés y en español. Pídame
  que abra cualquier parte y le muestro cómo funciona.

### Lines that must stay, and why

- **"This is an automated message" / "I'm not a person"** as the first line of every sample.
  Passing software off as a person is the thing regulators have gone after AI sellers for.
- **A sample label on anything with numbers.** It can be quiet, but it has to be there.
- **"I make no promise of jobs or money."** Your review names a case where the government sued a
  company selling AI to small businesses on earnings claims. This sentence is your distance from that.
- **One plain statement of what you can buy today.** Reworded as above, but present. A buyer who
  later learns he was the first, after a page that suggested otherwise, has a real complaint.
- **Monday to Saturday, 9 to 7; STOP stops everything; you approve every word; accounts in your
  name.** These come from Pennsylvania's telemarketing law and the texting rules. They are also
  selling points. Keep them prominent.
- **"I'm not a lawyer or an accountant."**
- **"No AI voice is used anywhere"** stays only while true. If the sample calls go on the site,
  change it to "sample calls: both voices are AI" in the same edit.
- **The site and the insurance application must describe the same business.** The quote was
  written for "written diagnostic reports". A site that sells installs under a policy for
  reports can give an insurer a reason to refuse a claim. Change them together.

## 3. Offer structure

### What the paperwork allows

| | Under your own name | Needs first |
|---|---|---|
| Free missed-call test, live demo on a call | Now | Nothing |
| Paid visit and written findings (no account access, nothing built) | After the errands | Own EIN (free, 15 minutes), separate bank account, the insurance decision, a short signed agreement |
| Any install on a client's accounts | No | Separate company formed (about $125), insurance in its name, the services agreement finished, the accountant's answer on sales tax and the free PA licence |
| Anything that texts, asks for reviews, or answers a phone | No | All of the above, plus the consent and recording wording |

On insurance: you decided to save the quote and not buy. Your own gate says bind it before the
first paid visit. I will not tell you it is unnecessary. For a report-only visit with liability
capped at the fee, the exposure is small. For an install that sends messages to a client's
customers, it is not small.

### Three shapes

**A. The visit, about $300. Sold in your own name.**
- Includes: 20-minute call, 90 minutes in the shop, the free response test, one page of written
  findings within two days, a 15-minute walk-through by phone. English or Spanish.
- Excludes: any account access, any build, the full Map with counted numbers and return visit.
- Time: about 3 hours of work plus driving. That is about $100 an hour before driving and about
  $65 with a 45-minute drive each way. Keep it to Wilkes-Barre, Scranton and Hazleton; Allentown
  by video or at a higher price.
- Needs a cut-down agreement. The Map agreement promises six deliverables and a refund formula
  built for eight hours of work. Do not sign that for a three-hour visit.

**B. Front desk setup, about $1,500. Sold by the new company.**
- Includes: website receptionist (fixed answers, says it is automated), booking with Approve /
  Decline on the owner's phone, confirmation and reminder emails in English and Spanish, pause
  switch and failure alerts, training, 30 days of fixes.
- Excludes: texting, the phone receptionist, building a website, review requests, monthly care.
- Client pays their own software bills directly. I am not sure of the monthly figure; check it
  before quoting.
- Time: the clone checklist says under 2 hours hands-on, and marks that as a target, not
  measured. Counting account setup with the owner, wording approval in two languages, connecting
  their website form, acceptance, the 7-day watch and handoff, I estimate 13 to 17 hours, and 20
  to 25 on the first two. That is $90 to $115 an hour later, $60 to $75 at first.

**C. Full office, about $2,500. Later.**
- B plus quote follow-ups, invoice reminders and the weekly numbers. These read the client's own
  spreadsheet, which is where the extra hours go.
- A monthly care plan of roughly $100 to $150 belongs here too. Without it you will do free
  support forever. Your binder's $500 a month does not fit a $1,500 install.

### Are the prices right?

- **$300: about right as a short visit, far too low for the full Map.** Eight hours for $300 is
  under $40 an hour, well below your own stop line of $100. As a three-hour visit it works, and it
  is a price a barber or a two-truck shop can say yes to without thinking.
- **$1,500: about right for shape B with the scope frozen. Too low if it includes texting, the
  phone receptionist or a website.** Expect to lose money on the first two.
- **$1,500 for a written report from someone with no customers was the harder sale.** Your binder
  already planned to drop it to $750 at day 90. Your instinct is ahead of the plan.
- I did not check these against what others in the area charge. That is judgment, not data.

### Prices on the site

Your earlier hard line was no price until the company is formed. I would overrule it for the
visit only. A hidden price makes a shop owner assume thousands; "$300, one visit, in writing"
does the opposite. Before it goes up: you can lawfully take the money (EIN, bank account), the
insurance decision is made, the visit agreement exists, and the scope on the site matches it
word for word. Keep the install price off the site until the company exists and one install has
been delivered and timed.

## Biggest warning

The wording is not the danger. The danger is that the $1,500 package feels small, so it gets sold
the easy way: in your own name, uninsured, before the company exists. The moment software you
installed sends a message to a client's customer, a mistake is yours personally, and it can reach
what you own in other businesses. Sell the $300 visit as soon as you like. Do not take a dollar
for an install until the company and its cover are in place.
